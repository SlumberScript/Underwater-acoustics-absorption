A B S T R A C T
This study proposes an underwater coating with sound absorption ability in the middle-to-low frequency range 
and establishes an acoustic theoretical model combining the equivalent medium theory and the transfer matrix 
method. The sound absorption coefficient, surface characteristic impedance, equivalent volume longitudinal 
wave modulus, and equivalent sound velocity are calculated and solved. Using the preset 20 sensitive parameters 
and the hypercube sampling method, this study establishes 100,000 random sound absorption coefficient curves 
in the frequency range of 1 Hz–1,000 Hz. Further, deep neural networks are employed to predict the average 
value of the sound absorption coefficient curve. The overall loss function is derived by combining the mean 
square error between the expected average sound absorption coefficient and its predicted value and the network- 
optimized loss function to ensure that the 20 sensitive parameters that meet the acoustic performance can be 
predicted. Finally, two randomly selected sound absorption curves are used for prediction tests. The verification 
results indicate that the error between the expected average absorption coefficient and the predicted average 
absorption coefficient corresponding to the 20 sensitive parameters is only 0.026 % and 0.33 %. The proposed 
method can be extended to predict the average absorption coefficient value for any acoustic structure, which 
could be beneficial for the performance development of acoustic functional devices.
1. Introduction
One of the main development directions in the field of acoustic 
metamaterials [1–3] in recent years has been how to apply artificial 
intelligence to design acoustic structures and materials on demand [4], 
and machine learning-based methods have undoubtedly been verified as 
one of the effective means [5–7]. Currently, machine learning is the 
mainstream method for solving artificial intelligence-related problems, 
and as an independent direction in the field, it is developing rapidly. The 
key to combining machine learning with acoustic metamaterials lies in 
developing and applying artificial intelligence-based algorithms that 
can enable machines to learn from sample databases and improve the 
ability of metamaterials to control sound waves. The applications of 
machine learning to the fields of speech recognition [8], computer 
vision [9], natural language processing [10], material behavior [11], 
non-destructive evaluation [12] and healthcare [13] have achieved 
notable success in recent years. The design process of acoustic meta­
materials involves many fields, including physics, acoustics, materials, 
and engineering. In the past, theoretical calculations and finite element 
methods faced the limitation that a structure defines the performance 
[14]. However, this approach includes numerous trials, has high error 
and labor costs, and requires much time and computational resources, 
which is unfavorable for the rapid optimization design of acoustic 
metamaterials.
Regarding the design of acoustic materials, various machine 
learning-based methods have been proposed for optimizing acoustic 
structures and materials, and they can be mainly divided into the 
following categories. Supervised learning is a subfield of the machine 
learning field, and a support vector machine (SVM) is a powerful su­
pervised learning-based model that has been widely used in classifica­
tion and regression tasks. The SVM can be used in the design of acoustic 
metamaterials to classify the acoustic properties of materials and to 
predict the impact of different structural parameters on a material’s 
performance, providing data-driven guidance for material design [15].

pre-labeled data to train models. On the contrary, it discovers hidden

Different from the above airborne acoustic metamaterials, the design 
of underwater acoustic metamaterials, especially for the design of un­
derwater coating, improving sound absorption performance is a chal­
lenging target due to underwater impedance mismatch and underwater 
pressure resistance limitations. In addition, due to the high production 
and experimental costs of underwater coating, accurate estimation of 
their acoustic properties is required before preparing experimental 
samples, especially predicting the structural and material parameters 
corresponding to the expected average sound absorption coefficient 
within a specific frequency band, which is a key factor. Previous studies 
employed the method of introducing prior knowledge into data-driven 
structural optimization at low frequencies and reduced the average 
acoustic transmission loss by 31.78 % compared to traditional imped­
ance gradient design [38]. In addition, machine learning-based and 
optimization algorithms were combined with finite element models to 
realize an accelerated reverse design of acoustic coatings in underwater 
sound attenuation applications; this approach increased the prediction 
speed of absorption coefficients by 4500 times and provided 
high-performance suboptimal solutions rapidly and efficiently. In earlier 
studies, artificial intelligence methods were not used to predict the 
average value of sound absorption performance. However, for the sound 
absorption performance of acoustic metamaterials, the average sound 
absorption coefficient in a certain frequency band represents the 
strength of their ability to absorb incident sound waves. This is of great 
help for the rapid and intelligent design of acoustic metamaterials.
Starting from predicting the structural dimensions and material 
properties of the underwater acoustic cover layer in the mid to low 
frequency range, especially within 1000 Hz, firstly, this study proposes a 
theoretical calculation method for an underwater coating structure with 
embedded multi-layer cavities and uses 100,000 sound absorption 
curves as a sample database. Secondly, 20 sensitive parameters are 
predicted based on the average value of a randomly selected sound 
absorption coefficient curve in the target frequency range of 1 Hz–1000 
Hz. Thirdly, it is shown that the average sound absorption coefficient 
corresponding to the predicted sensitive parameters has a very small 
error compared to the average value of the randomly selected sound 
absorption coefficient curve. Finally, the proposed machine learning- 
based method could accelerate the development process of underwa­
ter acoustic materials and provide a convenient means for underwater 
acoustic manipulation. This study is organized as follows. In section2, 
the structure and materials of underwater coating, as well as the 
calculation methods for acoustic properties, are presented in detail. 
Moreover, Section 2 gives out the preselected 100,000 representative 
sound absorption curves from the sample database. Section 3 presents 
deep learning-based framework and corresponding method. Section 4
provides the predicted results and errors for the average of any two 
absorption curves. Finally, we present conclusions in Section 5. The 
contribution of this study lies in its valuable reference for the rapid 
estimation and on-demand design of acoustic performance of under­
water coatings, which improves the development efficiency of acoustic 
performance of underwater functional devices. Machine learning 
methods to predict the inverse structural parameters of a given average 
sound absorption coefficient also provides assistance for the develop­
ment of more acoustic materials. It is a manifestation of the application 
of artificial intelligence methods in acoustic materials or structures.

Further, a sample database containing 16 sensitive geometric pa­
rameters and four sensitive material parameters directly affecting the 
sound absorption performance was constructed. These sensitive pa­
rameters included: (1) the thickness of each of the 10 layers in the un­
derwater coating di, which was 1 mm ≤di ≤20 mm, where 1 ≤i ≤10; 
(2) the diameter of the hollow part mi, which satisfied the condition of 
20 mm ≤mi ≤1980 mm, where i = 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9; (3) density ρ, which 
was 1E3 kg/m3 ≤ρ ≤1.5E3 kg/m3; (4) loss η, which satisfied the con­
dition of 0.1 ≤η ≤0.8; (5) Young’s modulus E, which was 1E7(1+ηi) Pa 
≤E ≤1E8(1+ηi) Pa; (6) Poisson’s ratio ν, which satisfied the condition 
of 0.4 ≤ν ≤0.49. A database sample within the frequency range of 1 
Hz–1000 Hz was established based on variables corresponding sensitive 
parameters. The more evenly distributed the data in the database were, 
the larger the range of data and the higher the accuracy of prediction 
were. For frequencies ranging from 1 Hz to 1000 Hz, 20 sensitive pa­
rameters in each group corresponded to a sound absorption curve. In 
addition, 100,000 different sound absorption curves were constructed, 
and the selection method for data establishment adopted the Latin hy­
percube sampling (LHS) method, which is a commonly used random 
stratified sampling method in multivariate parameter distributions. The 
distribution of 100,000 sets of row data is displayed in Fig. 6. Since it 
was challenging to achieve a sound absorption coefficient above 0.8 at 
frequencies of below 400 Hz, this study used the dataset in Fig. 6 for 
deep learning training.
3. Deep learning-based framework and method
The average value of 100,000 absorption coefficient curves was 
calculated, and 20 sensitive parameters were predicted based on the 
randomly selected average absorption coefficient. The frequency was 
specified to be in the range of 1 Hz–1000 Hz. The method of training the 
sample database adopted the DNN introduced in [4], which can be 
regarded as a mapping function between the input data and corre­
sponding output data. In this study, DNNs were used to estimate the 
corresponding sensitive parameters that could meet performance re­
quirements from a selected average sound absorption coefficient. 
Typically, before data are fed to a deep learning model’s input layer, it is 
necessary to normalize both the input and output data to accelerate the 
convergence speed of the model and improve network stability. After 
feeding the normalized input data to the model’s input, the network 
provided the predicted normalized output; then, the inverse normali­
zation was performed to obtain the non-normalized estimated value of 
the output data. It should be noted that the network input was the 
average sound absorption coefficient, whose value was from zero to one, 
so normalization was not necessary for these data; therefore, only the 
network output data, namely the structural parameters, were normal­
ized using the min-max normalization method, which maps data of 
different dimensions to the numerical range of [0,1].

the corresponding normalized true sensitive parameter data were 
denoted by sn, and the mapped neural network model was defined by 
f(⋅); then, the data mapping process could be simply expressed as sn =
f(α). For such types of mapping tasks, feed-forward, fully connected 
neural networks, which are also known as multi-layer perceptrons or 
directly referred to as DNNs, are commonly used. The DNNs are typically

5. Conclusion
This study introduces a DNN model with an improved loss function 
for predicting an average absorption coefficient curve. The overall loss 
function of the DNN model includes the original loss function aided with 
the MSE-based loss function. The MSE value is calculated between the 
expected average absorption coefficient and its predicted value, tar­
geting the 100,000 randomly generated absorption coefficient curves in 
the theoretical acoustic performance calculation model of underwater 
coating; this represents a sample database constructed using 20 sensitive 
parameters, including 16 geometric parameters and four material pa­
rameters. Considering that inputting a set of performance parameters in 
the development framework might correspond to multiple sets of 20 
sensitive parameters, using the original loss function could prevent 
DNNs from obtaining optimal results. Therefore, the on-demand design 
presented in this paper adds additional constraints to the DNN model to 
determine whether it can achieve the best unique results. By performing 
the prediction tests for two randomly selected absorption coefficient 
curves, the effectiveness of the proposed method is verified, demon­
strating very small errors of 0.026 % and 0.33 %. The work presented in 
this article could provide a valuable reference for accelerating the per­
formance design of underwater acoustic materials.
